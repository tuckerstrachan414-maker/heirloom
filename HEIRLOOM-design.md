# HEIRLOOM

*A small warm world where creatures inherit what kept their parents alive.*

---

## 1. What the game actually is

You seed a world with a few simple creatures. Then you leave them alone and watch.

They wander, eat, find mates, have babies, and die. Every baby is a slightly imperfect copy of its parents — most of the time it just inherits their traits, but sometimes something new appears. A thicker hide. A weird gut. Glowing skin.

Then the world hits them. Fire, ice, plague, a rock from space.

Most die. The ones that happen to have the right traits don't. Those survivors are now the whole species, and their traits are the new normal. Do that a hundred times and you get creatures you never designed — ash-eating burrowers, floating salt-drinkers, a thing that sleeps through apocalypses.

**You are not the player of a creature. You are the pressure.** Your job is to poke the world and see what crawls out.

The reference video is the "roll random traits, survive natural selection" format — that's the *feeling* to hit, but stretched across hundreds of generations instead of one creature's life.

---

## 2. The core loop

1. **Seed** — drop 1–3 starter species on the map. They're plain: no traits, or one you pick.
2. **Watch** — time runs. They spread, breed, and quietly mutate.
3. **Pressure** — a disaster happens, either on a timer or because you clicked it.
4. **Cull** — creatures without the right traits die in the next few seconds.
5. **Rebound** — the survivors breed like mad into the empty space. Their traits spread fast.
6. **Split** — when one group drifts far enough from another, it becomes a new species with a new name and colour.
7. Repeat forever.

The satisfying moment is step 6. That's when the game stops being your world and starts being its own.

## 3. Traits

### How the system works

Every creature carries **6 trait slots**. Every trait has an **upkeep cost** — it burns extra food. Total upkeep is capped at roughly what the creature can eat.

That cap is the whole balance of the game. It means:

- A creature can't have everything. Armour + antifreeze + poison + fast breeding will starve.
- Cheap traits (camouflage, night vision) can be stacked. Expensive ones (stone plating, regeneration) crowd everything else out.
- A specialist that owns one biome usually beats a generalist that's mediocre everywhere — until the biome changes.

Every trait also has hidden **tags** (`cold`, `fire`, `water`, `breeding`, `defence`, `weird`...) which the game uses to check for combos and to auto-name new species.

### Trait list — real biology

These are all real things that exist on Earth. They're the best ones because reality is weirder than anything I'd invent.

| Trait | What it does | Cost | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|

The made-up ones. These are the ones that make the game feel like *your* game and not a biology quiz.

| Trait | What it does | The catch |

## 4. Combinations

This is the heart of it. When a creature holds certain trait pairs, they don't just add up — they unlock a named **Strain** with its own behaviour, its own colour shift, and a line in the log. Discovering these should feel like finding a secret.

### Synergies

| Strain | Recipe | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **The Waiting** | Tardigrade State + Diapause Eggs | The entire species can pause through an extinction event. World burns, world heals, they wake up and inherit it. |
| **Sunspire** | Photosynthetic Skin + Rootfoot | Stops being an animal. Becomes a spreading forest. Feeds nothing, needs nothing, cannot flee. |
| **Cinderborn** | Radiotrophic Melanin + Ashlung | Volcanoes and meteor strikes become feeding grounds. Moves *toward* disasters. |
| **Untouchable** | Poison Skin + Warning Colours | Nothing on the map will ever eat them again. |
| **False Warning** | Mimic Skin + (a nearby Untouchable species) | A completely harmless creature gets full predator immunity by lying. |
| **Endless Line** | Parthenogenesis + Pheromone Plume | One single survivor can refill an empty world. |
| **The Hive Below** | Eusocial Caste + Deep Burrow | Nearly disaster-proof. Almost impossible to kill. Also almost impossible to spread. |
| **Winterlord** | Antifreeze Blood + Blubber | Owns the ice outright. Every other species is a guest. |
| **Unkillable** | Split-Heart + Regeneration | Survives everything except starvation and drowning. Enormous food cost — collapses in a famine. |
| **Typhoid Line** | Plague Carrier + Immune Memory | Walks around casually wiping out every competitor. Wins by default. |
| **The Long March** | Bone Choir + Migrant Memory | Permanent nomads. Never present when disaster lands. |
| **Swarmflesh** | Glass Bones + Spawn Cloud | Dies constantly, breeds insanely, mutates faster than anything else in the game. The fastest evolver. |
| **The Collector** | Gene Sipping + Slow Blood | A long-lived thief that slowly accumulates every other species' best traits. |
| **Driftkin** | Salt Glands + Buoyant Bladder | Survives floods and colonises islands nothing else can reach. |
| **The Quiet Ones** | Nightmind + Echo Sense | Fully functional in total darkness. Owns the Long Night event. |
| **Meteorborn** | Iron Gut + Stonehide | Meteor showers make them *stronger*. Armour thickens each strike. |
| **Boom and Burn** | Widow's Bloom + Ravenous | Chaos engine. Enormous booms, enormous crashes, wildest mutations in the world. |
| **Phoenix Line** | Ashbloom Seeds + Ashlung | Cannot breed without fire and cannot be harmed by it. Population is a graph of wildfires. |

### Anti-synergies (traits that fight each other)

Just as important. Evolution should be able to make mistakes.

- **Blubber + Heat Fins** — both burn energy doing opposite jobs. Net result: worse at everything.
- **Camouflage + Lek Display** — hiding and showing off. Neither works.
- **Camouflage + Warning Colours** — same conflict.
- **Rootfoot + anything movement-based** — the movement trait is dead weight upkeep.
- **Stonehide + Glass Bones** — mutually exclusive. Can't both exist in one body.
- **Parthenogenesis + Pheromone Plume**, oddly, is fine — but **Parthenogenesis + Immune Memory** is a trap: clones can't spread new immunity, so a mutated plague still ends them.

A species carrying an anti-synergy should get a slightly sickly colour tint so you can spot the doomed branches on the map.

## 5. Disasters and events

Three speeds, because a world where everything is sudden gets boring.

### Sudden — one moment, huge cull

- **Meteor Strike** — crater, ring of fire, then years of dust.
- **Volcanic Eruption** — lava tiles that stay permanently, ash spreading outward for a decade.
- **Wildfire** — spreads through dry grass and forest, stops at water. The most-used disaster.
- **Deluge** — low ground goes underwater for a few years.
- **Tsunami** — the entire coast, gone.
- **Lightning Storm** — random ignitions, kills tall creatures.
- **Hailstorm** — kills anything unarmoured in the open.
- **Earthquake** — chasms open. Uniquely, this one kills burrowers.
- **Solar Flare** — anything on the surface in daylight dies. Radiation lingers.
- **Acid Rain** — kills soft-skinned, spares armoured and burrowed.

### Slow — a squeeze over decades

- **Ice Age** — cold creeps in from the map edges over ~50 years and then slowly retreats.
- **The Long Summer** — water tiles shrink, rivers vanish, the map turns gold then rust.
- **Ash Winter** — sun blocked. Photosynthesis fails. Follows every eruption and meteor.
- **Famine** — food regrows at half speed.
- **The Rot** — a fungus that spreads across corpses and infects anything that touches them.
- **Sea Rise** — coasts slowly drown; islands appear and disappear.
- **Sinking** — a whole region subsides into swamp.

### Plagues — these are alive

A plague is not a timer. It's a tiny creature of its own with **its own traits and its own mutations**. It has a transmission rate, a lethality, and an incubation time, and those change over generations too.

That means: a plague that's too lethal burns out and dies with its hosts. One that mutates toward mildness survives forever as a background condition. The player gets to watch a disease evolve alongside the animals. This is the single best system in the game and it's cheap to build.

Named starting plagues:
- **Whisper Plague** — only infects social or herd species.
- **Cordyceps Bloom** — infected creatures walk toward healthy ones and burst.
- **Bloom of Glass** — silica dust, kills lungs unless the creature has Grit Lids.

### Good events (rare, and lovely)

- **Bloom** — food explosion. Everything breeds. Population doubles.
- **Blood Moon** — one night of universal mating success.
- **Iron Rain** — meteor shower that seeds metal into the ground. Food for Iron Gut lineages.
- **The Green Return** — after an ice age ends, the map heals in a wave you can watch move.

### Player-driven events

- **Invasive Species** — drop a foreign species in and see what happens.
- **The Culling** — spawn an apex predator.
- **Magnetic Reversal** — every Magnetic Sense creature loses its way at once. Migrations scatter across the map. Cruel and funny.
- **Gene Bolt** — force a mutation on one creature, personally. Your finger on the tree of life.

## 6. Species splitting and naming

When a group of creatures has drifted far enough from its parent species (say, 3+ trait differences held stably for a few generations), it splits off: new name, new colour, new branch on the family tree.

**Names are generated from traits**, so they describe what happened:

- prefix from the dominant trait: *Ash-, Frost-, Stone-, Salt-, Glass-, Night-, Root-, Iron-, Ember-, Deep-, Drift-*
- suffix from body plan or behaviour: *-delver, -kin, -wing, -crawler, -bloom, -hide, -mother, -spine, -walker, -husk*

So a burrowing ash-breather becomes the **Ashdelvers**. A floating salt-drinker becomes the **Driftkin**. A rooted photosynthesiser becomes the **Rootbloom**.

A **family tree screen** shows the whole branching history with the year each split happened and what disaster caused it. That screen is the thing people screenshot.

## 7. Look and feel

**Pixel art, top-down, chunky tiles.** Warm palette — cream, gold, peach, rust, teal. No dark neon.

- Sand: cream. Grass: gold. Forest: deep green-gold. Water: teal. Clay: rust. Ash: pale grey. Ice: near-white with a blue tint. Lava: hot orange against the rust.
- Creatures are tiny — 4 to 6 pixels — and **drawn from their traits**. Stonehide adds grey plating pixels. Photosynthetic Skin adds green. Lek Display adds a bright crest. Glass Bones makes them pale and translucent. Evolution is something you *see*, not something you read in a menu.
- Camera zooms from whole-world down to a handful of creatures.

**The tone is calm.** This is a nature documentary, not an action game. Soft ambient background, gentle time passing, and then a disaster hits with a screen shake and a colour wash — fire tints everything rust, ice drains it to white, ash goes grey.

**The log at the bottom is the narrator.** Short, dry, documentary lines:

> Year 214. Eruption in the north.
> The Ashdelvers survived. Three of forty remain.
> Year 219. All three carry Ashlung.
> Year 240. The Ashdelvers have retaken the north. There is no one left to compete with them.

That log is doing enormous emotional work for almost no effort. Prioritise it.

## 8. Modes

1. **Sandbox** — no goals. Seed, watch, poke. The default and the main mode.
2. **Gauntlet** — 500 years of escalating disasters, automatic. Score = generations survived, species alive, strains discovered.
3. **Trials** — small designed puzzles. *"Survive the ice age with only 4 trait slots."* *"Get a species onto the far island."* *"Make a species that requires fire to breed and keep it alive 100 years."*
4. **Two Worlds** — you seed one species, the game seeds a rival, and you both start from nothing. Competition without direct control.

Ship with Sandbox and Gauntlet. Add the others later.

## 9. Building it — the honest version

Everything here is doable as one self-contained HTML file that runs on your phone and a school Chromebook, with no outside anything. But some parts are cheap and some are expensive.

**Cheap (build first):**
- Tile map, biomes, drawing — straightforward.
- Creature wandering, eating, breeding, dying — simple rules per creature.
- Traits as a list of effects on numbers (speed, food need, cold tolerance).
- Disasters as tile changes plus a survival check.
- The narrator log.
- Trait-based creature sprites.

**Expensive (build second, and only once the cheap stuff feels good):**
- Species splitting and the family tree.
- Living, mutating plagues.
- The full combo/Strain system.
- Predator behaviour beyond "sometimes eats".

**Where it will break, and what to do about it:**

- **Too many creatures.** Phones will choke past roughly 500–600 at once. Cap the population and let food scarcity enforce it. Don't try to simulate 5,000 things.
- **Too many traits.** 50 traits sounds great and is unbalanceable and unreadable. **Start with about 25 traits and 10 combos.** Get those feeling right, then add. I'd rather cut half this document than ship a mush.
- **Nothing interesting happens.** The most likely failure. If mutation is too rare or disasters are too weak, you watch beige creatures wander for ten minutes. Fix: mutation chance around 8% per birth, and a disaster every 20–40 years minimum. Tune toward *too much chaos* first, then dial back.
- **Everything dies immediately.** The other failure. Disasters should kill 60–90% and leave a seed population, never 100%. Build in a hard rule: a disaster never removes the last handful of a species outright.
- **You can't tell what's going on.** Solve with the log and with colour. If a species is thriving, its colour should be spreading visibly across the map.

**Save system:** a copy-paste text code, not a file. Works everywhere, including school machines.

**Speed controls:** 1× / 4× / 16× / pause. Essential — evolution is boring at real speed and thrilling at 16×.

## 10. What I'd build first

A one-screen version:

- 60×40 tile map, three biomes
- 100 creatures, 8 traits, 1 disaster (wildfire)
- The narrator log
- 4× speed

If a wildfire rolls across gold grass and thirty seconds later every survivor happens to have Ashlung and you *felt* that happen — the game works, and the rest is just adding more. If it doesn't feel like anything, no amount of extra traits will save it.

Start there.
